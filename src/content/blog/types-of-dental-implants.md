Missing teeth affect more than just your smile. They can impact your ability to chew comfortably, your speech, and even the long-term health of your jawbone. Dental implants are widely considered the gold standard for tooth replacement because they look, feel, and function like natural teeth. ## What Is a Dental Implant?

A dental implant is a small titanium post that is surgically placed into the jawbone to serve as an artificial tooth root. Once the implant fuses with the bone through a process called osseointegration, a crown is attached on top, creating a permanent, natural-looking replacement tooth.

## The Three Main Types of Implants

### 1. Endosteal Implants
These are the most common type of dental implant and the standard of care in modern dentistry.
- Placement: Surgically inserted directly into the jawbone.
- Material: Typically made of titanium, shaped like small screws.
- Best for: Patients with adequate healthy jawbone density.
- Healing time: Three to six months for osseointegration before the crown is placed.

### 2. Subperiosteal Implants
These sit on top of the jawbone but under the gum tissue.
- Placement: Placed under the gum but on or above the jawbone, not inside it.
- Best for: Patients who do not have enough healthy jawbone and cannot or prefer not to undergo a bone grafting procedure.
- Less common today due to advances in bone augmentation techniques.

### 3. Zygomatic Implants
The least common type, used in complex cases.
- Placement: Anchored into the cheekbone (zygoma) instead of the jawbone.
- Best for: Patients with severe jawbone loss who are not candidates for traditional implants or bone grafting.
- Requires a highly skilled oral surgeon, as this is the most complex implant procedure.

## Implant Materials

Most implants are made from one of two materials:
- Titanium, the most widely used material, proven safe and biocompatible over decades of clinical use.
- Zirconia, a ceramic alternative for patients with metal sensitivities or allergies, though less commonly available.

## Bone Grafting: Building a Foundation

If your jawbone is too thin or soft to support an implant, a bone graft may be needed before placement. This involves adding bone material to the jaw to create a solid foundation. Healing from a bone graft typically takes four to six months before the implant can be placed.
