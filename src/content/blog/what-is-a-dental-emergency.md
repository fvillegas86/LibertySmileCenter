Dental emergencies can happen at any time, whether during sports, while eating, or even in the middle of the night. Knowing how to identify one and what steps to take can mean the difference between saving and losing a tooth. ## Common Dental Emergencies

### Knocked-Out Tooth (Avulsed Tooth)
This is one of the most critical dental emergencies. If a permanent tooth is knocked out, time is of the essence. You have roughly 30 minutes to save it.
- Keep the tooth moist at all times. Handle it by the crown, not the root.
- If possible, gently rinse the tooth and try placing it back in the socket.
- If that is not possible, store it between your cheek and gums, or in a container of milk.
- Call your dentist immediately.

### Severe Toothache
A persistent, throbbing toothache can indicate an infection, abscess, or deep decay reaching the nerve. Do not ignore it.
- Rinse your mouth with warm salt water.
- Use dental floss to gently remove any trapped food.
- Apply a cold compress to the outside of your cheek if there is swelling.
- Avoid placing aspirin directly on the gums, as it can burn the tissue.

### Chipped or Broken Tooth
Save any pieces if you can. Rinse your mouth and the broken pieces with warm water. If there is bleeding, apply gauze to the area for about 10 minutes or until it stops. A small chip may not be painful, but a large break can expose the nerve and requires prompt treatment.

### Lost Filling or Crown
A lost filling or crown can leave your tooth sensitive and vulnerable to further damage. Keep the area clean and avoid chewing on that side. If you still have the crown, you can temporarily reattach it with dental cement from a pharmacy, but see your dentist as soon as possible.

### Abscess or Swelling
An abscess is a serious infection that appears as a pimple-like swelling on the gums. It can spread quickly and become life-threatening if left untreated. Signs include severe pain, swelling, fever, and a bad taste in your mouth. This requires immediate dental attention.

## When to Go to the Emergency Room

If your dental injury involves severe facial trauma, uncontrollable bleeding, or difficulty breathing or swallowing, go to the nearest hospital emergency room immediately.
